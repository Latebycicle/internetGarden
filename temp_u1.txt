Patch clamp experiments- With tiny electrodes (micropipettes filled with
electrolyte and containing an electrode), we can record electrical activity
in single neurons.
The patch-clamp technique utilizes a glass micropipette, which is patched
onto the cell membrane surface to electrically isolate a small membrane
area for recording ion channel activity. 9 10 A small suction is applied to the
back of the pipette, forming a high-resistance seal in the giga-ohm range,
known as a gigaseal, which ensures that all current flows through the
pipette without leaking through other membrane regions.

A synapse is a specialised contact between axon and dendrite of two
neurons. A spike causes neurotransmitter release at the synapse, which in
turn changes the potential of the receiving neuron.
The strength of a synapse can vary: it depends on amount of transmitter
released and number of postsynaptic receptors.
The weight of a synapse may change, and it’s affect is directly proportional
to the weight.
Vesicles contain chemicals (neurotransmitters) required for signalling.
Signalling is a stochastic process—it is not very reliable.
If you record two events from the same synapse, they will never look
exactly the same.

Excitatory and Inhibitory Synapses

Neurons

5

Synapses can excite (bring closer to spiking threshold), or inhibit the
receiving neuron.
Dale’s principle: Each neuron can make only either excitatory or inhibitory
synapses.
A neuron typically receives both excitatory and inhibitory inputs.
An excitatory synapse excites a neuron, so it will increase the membrane
potential—it will bring it closer to the threshold.
An inhibitory synapse inhibits the neuron, making it less likely to fire as it
reduces the membrane potential.

The brain creates and eliminates synapses
A typical adult cortical neuron has around 10,000 synapses.
During early development (synaptogenesis) and beyond, neurons and
synapses are overproduced and eliminated.
Cell loss: 25-40% or more (Finlay & Pallas, 1989).
Collateral/synapse loss: ubiquitous (Purves & Lichtmann, 1980); in primate
visual cortex alone around 5000 synapses/second are eliminated during
adolescence (Bourgeois & Rakic, 1993).
This is called pruning and has been linked to circuit refinement and
specification.

Summary
Neurons transmit information through spikes: electrical all-or-non events.
Synapses mediate communication between neurons through chemical
neurotransmitters.
Synapses have either an excitatory or inhibitory effect.
Synapses have different strength or weight, which quantifies their influence
on the receiving neuron.
We can use highly simplified neuron models to investigate neural
computations.
